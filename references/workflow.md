# Stable IELTS Learning Workflow

## Separate rules from state

Maintain four distinct layers:

- **Final target:** examination window, required overall score, and any minimum component scores.
- **Stable rules:** evidence, teaching, feedback, and recording principles.
- **Rolling plan:** natural-month aim, natural-week ambition, and daily target.
- **Evidence:** original answers, prompting level, elapsed time, study reports, and review results.

Changing progress updates the rolling plan, not the stable rules.

## Core principles

1. Start from existing evidence instead of reconstructing progress from memory.
2. Give each study period one primary objective; secondary objectives are observations, not competing tasks.
3. Define the expected output, allowed prompting, and success criteria before the task.
4. Teach necessary methods and callable language before demanding open output.
5. Validate transfer with unfamiliar material.
6. Preserve the learner's self-assessment alongside the teacher or agent assessment. Resolve disagreement through later evidence.
7. Address information accuracy and task function before language polish.
8. Do not use question volume, repetition, or a filled schedule as a proxy for learning.
9. Keep a dated study report after every substantive session.

## Planning hierarchy

### Final target

Record the exam date or window and score requirements. If the date is not booked, label any working date as a planning anchor.

### Monthly aim

Use natural months and describe capability milestones rather than daily exercises. Keep meaningful capacity unallocated for fatigue, schedule changes, teacher lessons, and unresolved problems.

### Weekly Ambition

Use natural weeks. Define a small number of observable outcomes and how they will be verified. Do not assume weekends or public holidays are available.

### Daily target

Choose from the Weekly Ambition after checking the learner's calendar, latest report, current time, fatigue, materials, and prerequisites. When extra time appears, pull from an existing schedulable-task list rather than inventing unrelated work.

## Session boundary

Before studying, determine:

- prior-task completion based on learner report and observable evidence;
- whether the week is ahead, on track, or behind;
- the day's usable time, fatigue, materials, and dependencies;
- whether the learner wants to reschedule the task;
- what happens to a replaced or cancelled task.

Ask only questions whose answers would materially change the session.

## Task loop and completion branches

Use the default loop: define target, give necessary input, run a short controlled application, obtain lower-prompt output, assess, and branch.

- **Primary and secondary objectives achieved:** close the task and move on.
- **Primary achieved, secondary unstable:** close the main task and classify the remaining issue as blocking, natural-recurrence, low-impact observation, or closed.
- **Primary and secondary objectives not achieved:** diagnose from the answer, ask where the learner is stuck, teach the intersection precisely, and validate once more when useful.

One or two validations are a default, not an absolute limit. Stop when continued work has poor returns.

## Attention checks

Use a brief check only when there is a real choice between continuing, reducing load, switching, resting, or stopping. Sample comparable material from previously learned content. Do not interpret topic difficulty as an attention decline. Record the result only when it changes the plan.

## Weekly and monthly review

A Weekly Review records achieved evidence, stable abilities, blocking issues, natural-recurrence items, cancelled or replaced tasks, next week's ambition, and schedulable tasks. Old problems occupy the next session only when they block the new plan.

A Monthly Review checks the final target, component trends, calendar capacity, and the next month's milestones. Change long-term ability judgments only from repeated evidence.

## Learning modes

In direct Codex study, the agent organizes the task, teaching, assessment, adaptation, and record. In human-teacher collaboration, the learner debriefs lesson content, teacher judgment, completed materials, understanding, and assignments. The agent updates self-study and prepares a two-way summary without silently overruling either person.

## Subject routing

- Writing needs explicit task understanding, organization, controlled language, independent transfer, and preservation of original work.
- Reading and Listening may use targeted error removal instead of whole-subject teaching when the learner already has a stable high-scoring method.
- Speaking should reduce disruptive pauses and unfinished structures before prioritizing memorized complexity. Use brief repair and new-topic transfer.

## Minimum records and revision space

Daily reports and Weekly Reviews are required. Lesson plans, specialist reviews, and attention-test records are optional unless they affect future decisions.

Treat evidence-first planning, predefined objectives, concise feedback loops, learner scheduling authority, and required study reports as the stable core. Treat workload, validation count, subject allocation, review timing, and lesson-plan detail as configurable. Treat dates, targets, fatigue, current weaknesses, and teacher input as dynamic state.
